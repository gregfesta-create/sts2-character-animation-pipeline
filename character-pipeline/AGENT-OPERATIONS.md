## Required artwork approval

### Layered skeletal preparation

Use the approved drawing as the design source for articulated 2D parts. Separate limbs, wings, body, head and equipment where the intended motion needs independent joints. Reconstruct the complete underlying body and joint overlap before animating; moving an extracted wing must not expose the hole it left behind. Preserve the approved detailed appearance and full-character proportions. Do not substitute global image warping for changing poses.

The user-facing input is an image and the requested character behavior. Do not ask the user to author technical masks, name bones, place pivots, assign weights or write the structured specification. Those are responsibilities of the operating AI. Preserve the original-art approval checkpoint, then carry preparation and rigging through automatically where supported. Report a concrete capability failure if an available tool cannot complete a required step; do not quietly substitute an already separated example or claim that supplying a skeleton specification satisfies image-to-rig acceptance. A full unprepared-image-to-rig demonstration remains required before calling this workflow finished.

The operating AI owns semantic layer planning, reconstruction through the existing artwork providers, and visual inspection. Inspect isolated layers, the rest composition and the widest intended poses. Check shoulders, hips and wing roots for gaps, detached fragments, wrong overlap and exposed unfinished surfaces. Repair the layers and repeat the affected preview before calling the motion acceptable. Coverage and geometry checks alone do not establish correct anatomy or animation quality. Original changes still require human approval; technical preparation of an already approved design does not introduce another routine approval stop.

Preserve connected anatomy and decoration when partitioning an image. A broad polygon boundary can cut one ribbon, feather, sleeve or cable into independently moving fragments. Identify its semantic owner and keep the continuous structure under the same motion, or reconstruct a complete attachment before separating it. Inspect changing poses rather than only a rest recomposition, and do not reduce motion to conceal a preparation defect. A layer rigidly rotating on one bone demonstrates layer movement; it does not establish articulated joints, movable baked-in legs, or a naturally bending wing.

For a part with reconstructed or previously occluded areas, author an internal `requiredSurfaceMask: {"path": "body-required.png"}` declaration. Every nonzero grayscale mask pixel requires nonzero finished alpha. Use the exact pre-crop part raster dimensions, including individual OpenRaster layer dimensions rather than the archive canvas; prepared inputs use their current raster dimensions. This mask describes the complete required surface, not the pixels to erase or repaint. Never shrink it to make an incomplete reconstruction pass. An empty mask or dimension mismatch is a preparation failure. Keep image generation at source resolution separate from combat-size registration; do not distort the character to satisfy a generation model's size constraint.

The preparation receipt binds the source, required mask and final prepared PNG hashes. Its coverage status measures occupied pixels only. Parts without this declaration have no required-surface proof. The technical mask does not require the user to approve the original again; changes to actual original artwork still do. When coverage fails, reconstruct the missing surface through the available image tools and resume the affected preparation stage. When pixels pass but anatomy or motion looks wrong, repair the artwork or rig rather than treating coverage as visual approval.

Keep source raster pixels separate from combat canvas units during generated-part preparation. Repainting derives its size from the captured part image; reference-based part generation uses the normalized approved reference raster. The provider pads only the right and bottom to a supported multiple of eight (minimum64, maximum4096 per axis and16million pixels), then crops back to the exact source extent. It does not resample the image or move its pivot. Paint-mask padding stays unpainted, and repaint composites against the original image and original-sized mask to preserve untouched RGBA pixels. Source/padded sizes and policy identity belong to the generation checkpoint. Oversized sources fail explicitly rather than silently changing character scale. Reference generation can still redraw anatomy; inspect its registration before rigging.

The selected approach is 2D skeletal animation. The reference mod's locally inspected embedded scripts instantiate SpineSprite, reuse the displayed skeleton data, sample animations, alter bone matrices and mesh deformation, and reorder rendered parts. This establishes its runtime technique, not its original authoring process or permission to reuse its implementation. Our pipeline should produce original articulated artwork with comparable movement; a full 3D backend is outside the current scope.

Keep spawned command windows hidden. The shared process launcher already uses `shell: false` and `windowsHide: true`; any separately launched Windows background helper must also be hidden. Do not open editors or browsers as an incidental validation step.

1. Build or resume the job to obtain its artwork review result. Read the exact current `reviewId` and original preview paths from that result or `status JOB_DIRECTORY`.

## Artwork providers

### Fit a scaffold to explicit landmarks

Before adding prepared parts, animation clips, sockets, components, visual states, polylines, projectiles or assembly descriptors, the operating AI can fit an initial scaffold to explicit canonical canvas pixel landmarks:

```text
node character-pipeline/cli.js fit-rig SPEC.json --landmarks LANDMARKS.json --out FITTED.json --config tools.json
```

The landmark declaration is `{ "version": 1, "sourceSpecHash": "SHA256", "bones": [{ "id": "root", "start": [256, 300], "end": [256, 240] }] }`, with exactly one entry for every bone. Coordinates use the specification's rightward X, downward Y canvas convention and must be within +/-1000000 pixels. Choose landmarks explicitly from the intended character anatomy; the command does not infer joints or supply guessed defaults. Existing nonempty dependent collections are refused, because moving a rig beneath authored meshes, mounts or clips would invalidate their placement.

`sourceSpecHash` is the canonical hash of the **complete parsed specification**, not a hash of raw JSON file bytes. In a Node authoring script, obtain it with `require('./character-pipeline/lib/assets').hashValue(JSON.parse(require('node:fs').readFileSync(specPath, 'utf8').replace(/^\uFEFF/, '')))`, run from the repository root. Retain stable IDs and topology when selecting landmarks. The command computes parent-local transforms, unit positive scales, segment lengths and shifted limits that preserve each joint's rest-angle slack. It refuses numerically unrepresentable results instead of accepting a visibly incorrect placement.

`FITTED.json` must be a fresh path inside the configured output root. Both inputs remain unchanged; the complete validated output increments the specification revision. This is specification authoring only: it opens no job, reads no artwork pixels, creates no preview and grants no artwork approval. Build still pauses at the original-art approval gate before conversion or compiled rig creation. This command does not establish animation quality or anatomical correctness.

This budget does not prescribe how many drawings an animation needs.

Optional interpolation tools require visual review before their frames become accepted artwork. Check chest, face, feet, feather tips, silhouette and transparency through the entire interval, especially where occlusion changes. Reject duplicated anatomy, disappearing limbs, ghost edges or matte halos; retain the source drawings and use coherent new drawings for unsupported gaps. A black-matte interpolation experiment is not a transparent runtime asset or proof of an articulated rig. Keep portable executables/models outside the published source and runtime pack.

Animated elemental details belong in separate partial effect layers when the base effect must remain visible. The trusted native overlay catalog supports either the existing `{texture,offset,scale}` presentation or `{frames,durations,offset,scale,loop}` for a bounded flipbook. Frames are already loaded `Texture2D` resources, not paths supplied by gameplay facts. Use 2–64 equally sized frames with at most 16,777,216 cumulative pixels, matching positive frame durations from 1/240 second and a total duration of at most 10 seconds. The owning scene advances the monotonic native clock. Repeated unchanged active observations preserve animation phase; a new projectile starts its own phase. Nonloops hold the last frame. Existing pause behavior hides effects and restarts their presentation when resumed; it does not change captured attack selections.

Select flame, ember, poison or ice overlay IDs from the existing authoritative rules and pre-consumption attack snapshot. An overlay alone does not recolor the wind/base texture. Automatic game event callers and artistic acceptance require their own verified integration; rendering a native flipbook does not establish them.

For normal approved-resource builds, an overlay may add `presentation.animation` with exact `{columns,rows,frameCount,durations,loop}` fields alongside its existing offset and scale. Supply one approved PNG sheet through the existing resource reference. The row-major grid must contain exactly 2–64 frames and divide the verified image dimensions evenly. Frame count is a bound, not a recommended animation density. Durations use the same native bounds above; the compiler embeds atlas frames referencing one stored texture. Frame paths, arbitrary shaders, import overrides and approval flags are not accepted in this descriptor. Changing the descriptor changes capability/cache identity. Static overlay requests remain valid.

The incoming clip owns drawing selection during transform blends. Missing tracks use group defaults, loops wrap, nonloops clamp, death holds its endpoint and idle/cancellation returns to idle selections. Controller seeking is silent and reconstructs selection at the target time. Use the character controller APIs; raw mixer edits are synchronized only at controller boundaries. State eligibility, pose selection and action hiding compose independently, so clearing a buff state or releasing a nocked projectile cannot reveal an inactive drawing. Compiled preview captures apply the same selection function. Bone-only telemetry remains bone-only evidence; actual native frames establish visible drawing selection.

### Authored deformation preflight

Every rig compiler candidate, including each automatic repair candidate, first passes an authored-data geometry projection. `rig.json` and the build result expose `authoredGeometry` separately from native validation. Its `specHash` identifies the exact compiled candidate and must match `repair.repairedSpecHash` after repair. Source artwork approval is unchanged. This check does not create artwork or require another human checkpoint.

For a supported sampled authored triangle collapse or inversion, the repair driver independently recomputes the current candidate's diagnosis before authorizing `dampenClipPose`. It changes position, rotation and scale deviations from rest by exactly one half for one clip per action, preserving timing, easing, events, source art, mesh weights and other clips. Successive candidates use distinct source hashes; the original input remains immutable and repaired specification/history are persisted. Unsafe scale sign crossings, invalid rest geometry, unsupported or ambiguous authored data, native-only registration/deformation failures and exhausted correction budgets remain explicit failures. Native mismatches never authorize this authored correction. A sequence using damping has receipt scope `authored-local-animation`; loop-only repair retains `authored-local-loop-pose`. Source-free resume verifies and reuses approved captured assets and completed evidence.

The projection uses explicit mesh topology and weights, prepared-image pivots and pixels-per-unit, full hierarchical affine rest frames, inverse-rest skinning, and authored position/rotation/scale values. Untracked properties use rest values. Exact keys and outgoing-key linear, in, out, inOut and step interpolation are supported for rotation, position and scale; step holds the outgoing value until the next exact key, and non-loop clips hold the last key. Easing changes timeline progress; deformation remains weighted linear skinning. Times before the first key, loop endpoints/wrap, and poses outside runtime joint limits are reported as omissions. This is bounded sampled evidence, not a continuous geometric proof. Sprite quads without an explicit mesh are uncovered. Native float precision, rendered pixels, loop seams, blends, runtime actions/constraints and assembled component behavior remain outside this receipt's scope.

Sampling selects the first 32 clips with explicit omitted clip IDs (preserving native non-rendered support for 64 clips), and uses the shared 16 fps planner with at most 32 times per clip, then a global cap of 128 motion times, 8,192 projected vertices and 16,384 triangles including rest. Key/event omissions, actual sampled coordinates and signed areas retain clip/time/part/triangle mappings. Supported sampled collapse or orientation reversal rejects before the compiler runs; there are no inversion exceptions or invented motion-quality tolerances. Rest-only/no-mesh coverage is `unverified`; omitted coverage is `partial`. A sampled `passed` result never certifies visual quality or native deformation. Input and receipt serialization are bounded at 16 MiB and 2 MiB respectively.

Use absolute artwork paths when writing definitions into a separate output directory. Do not add output directories to artwork roots merely to load JSON. Captured component definitions and originals support resume after their source files have been removed; resume verifies captured bytes instead of reopening those originals. Library callers of `inspectComponentGraph` may supply a trusted `definitionRoots` list (at most 33 entries, for 32 source roots plus output); omission preserves the existing source-roots-only behavior. This option is not specification JSON authority and does not change CLI root-path rebasing.

### Apply a revision to an existing job

Use `modify SPEC.json --patch PATCH.json --job JOB_DIRECTORY --config tools.json` for a patch plus rebuild. The specification, patch and job must follow configured path containment; the specification and its prior-revision backup must be owned outputs outside the job directory. Supply the exact specification currently stored in that job. Stale/wrong jobs fail `STALE_JOB_CONTEXT` (exit 2) before revision persistence. A fresh exclusive `SPEC.json.revision-N.json` backup prevents concurrent revisions from overwriting one another. The result includes revision paths and `build`, including `awaiting_artwork_approval` when required.

The helper retains source captures, effects and rendering settings. Unchanged originals keep their approval for animation-only edits. Generated-original inputs or relevant artwork/style changes require approval again. Component topology or assembly changes recapture current sources through the normal typed root policy; retain the current component definitions/artwork for those changes. Plain `modify` without `--job` continues to author a revision only.

If rebuild fails after persistence, read the bounded `error.recovery` fields; do not report a successful rebuild. Preserve both the revised specification and its backup. Inspect `status JOB_DIRECTORY`: if the committed job specification equals the saved revision, `resume` can recover it. ### Trusted combat presentation caller

Read current facts again after consumption with `ObserveCurrent`; released projectiles keep their captured state. Forward actual target updates, impacts, completion, cancellation and monotonic clocks through the caller. Dispose on the main thread. Projectile-only roots use empty binding IDs and empty facts. General nonprojectile gestures and owner lifecycle subscriptions still need explicit real-mod integration.

The source-to-animation inventory is an operating-AI task: inspect implementations and branches before choosing shared clips. Validation checks source evidence and real clip references; it does not decide card semantics by itself. For automatic attack-specific additions, generated kits expose `AttachCombatWithActionFactsReady`, forwarding to `CombatPresentationCaller.AttachReadyWithActionFacts`. ## Preparing attacks before queued playback

For cosmetic companions, check BaseLib discovery from the actual `NCreature`, not just its `Visuals`. A nested companion tree can capture the hero's triggers even when the hero has an animation player. Preserve the primary handler and isolate independently controlled companion visuals from that discovery subtree, with explicit ownership, transform, visibility and pause handling. Tie queued presentation to actual owner death and combat lifetime; revival requires a fresh root after terminal disposal. See the [public technical acceptance summary](release/ACCEPTANCE.md) for the exercised integration and its limits.

When gameplay resolves another attack before its previous animation finishes, capture its modifiers immediately with `caller.PrepareBeforeConsumption(actionId, clipId, target, releaseAt, impactAt)`. The returned `CombatPresentationCaller.PreparedAction` contains immutable request metadata; its copied facts stay private to the caller. Preparation starts no native attack. Continue the actual gameplay command without a presentation-owned delay.

When the bounded presentation queue reaches that action, call `caller.BeginPrepared(handle)`. This starts from the captured facts without invoking gameplay readers again. The request's sequence identifies capture; delivery uses a fresh monotonic native observation sequence so intervening passive observations cannot invalidate a legitimate queued snapshot. Correlate real hits by action, generation and released source identity, not by assuming the two sequences are equal.

Handles belong to one caller, are single-use and have an `IsTerminal` state. At most 32 may be pending. Use `DiscardPrepared` for a queued action that will not start; use normal cancellation for an already-started action. Disposal and factory-root exit invalidate pending handles. Capacity, capture or start failure is a presentation diagnostic, never a reason to repeat damage or change buff consumption. Existing `BeginBeforeConsumption` remains available for immediate playback.

## Confirmed-hit impact overlays

A normal first-party effect entry may include `impact: { resourceId, durationSeconds, presentation }`. The impact resource is an exact approved original from the request's `resources`, with the same approval/hash/style checks as projectile resources. `presentation` accepts overlay `offset`/`scale` and the existing optional atlas `animation`; it does not accept enemy tint or executable shaders. Duration must be positive and at most ten seconds. Projectile and impact resources, transforms and durations are independent. Existing requests without impacts retain their behavior.

For example, a selected fire effect may declare an impact duration of 0.85 seconds and a selected poison effect 1.2 seconds. Separate channels can select both. These are authored example values, not global animation defaults. The normal compiler embeds the approved static/atlas resources and reports actual impact configuration. A private procedural preview script is not a substitute for that capture/compiler/export path.

Bind confirmed hits with the actual released source cue when available:

```csharp
caller.ConfirmImpact(actionId, hitCueId, releaseCueId, actionClock, hitWorldPosition);
```

`hitCueId` identifies the game-confirmed hit; `releaseCueId` identifies its actual released projectile cue. They need not be equal. The bridge retains copied selected IDs per socket before consumption, then uses the confirmed source's socket. It never reads current buffs again at impact. This prevents a poisoned arrow from receiving an unrelated magic projectile's modifiers within the same action. The legacy four-argument overload can infer optional impact effects only when the actually released sources share one socket; ambiguous legacy hits omit optional impact graphics and record a diagnostic.

Only a successful authoritative hit starts impact overlays. Advancing time, reaching a target, releasing a projectile or consuming a charge does not imply a hit. Cancellation or disposal during a hit callback prevents allocation afterward. Quota or optional graphics failure does not turn a confirmed gameplay hit into failure or trigger a gameplay retry. Complete an attack after its required confirmed hits; this extension preserves existing action-retention rules rather than reopening closed actions.

Impacts own new fixed-world anchors and overlay nodes, never the enemy's node, transform, tint, health or status. Effects expire independently through the existing monotonic presentation clock. Completion allows started short effects to finish; cancellation, combat end and disposal clean up their owned nodes. Enemy following, dedicated hit-reaction anatomy and real mod hit scheduling still require explicit integration. The private Sylphie test's floating damage value and receiver flash are scripted examples, not STS2 damage computation.
